5. Favoring a tool is different from trusting its answers.

PDF edition page 6

Among people already using AI, which survey answer better distinguishes daily from less-frequent use: favorability toward using AI, or trust? We compare these answers within 2025 and within 2026, using the trust item asked in each year.

REPORTED BEHAVIOR

How often do you use it?

The primary analyses compare daily with less-frequent use among 26,102 current users in 2025 and 14,158 in 2026, each with complete use, attitude and trust answers.

FAVORABLE STANCE

How do you feel about using it?

The favorability item asks about using AI in the development workflow in 2025 and AI tools at work in 2026. It is one broad evaluation, not a validated psychological scale. It may reflect usefulness, fit or experience; these explanations are not separately established.

TRUST

What kind of trust is being asked about?

The 2025 question concerns output accuracy. The 2026 question concerns task scope and conditions for reliance. Neither is interchangeable with favorability, actual verification behavior or measured work quality.

Why restrict the main analysis to current users? It avoids making the result mainly a comparison of users with nonusers. It does not eliminate the conceptual closeness between favoring use and reporting use. [3]

Trust still added something.

In a separate repeated-validation check, adding attitude to context plus trust improved mean AUC by 0.090 in 2025 and 0.061 in 2026. Adding trust to context plus attitude improved it by 0.003 and 0.012. All gains were positive in all 50 matched folds. Context plus attitude also beat context plus trust in all 50 folds in each year. Overlapping folds are not 50 independent replications.

The attitude-over-trust ordering also held in each year's professional subset and country-grouped validation. Those checks strengthen the within-dataset finding; they do not establish universal applicability or a causal effect. The larger 2026 trust increment does not prove that trust became more important.

9. A strong association is not an adoption lever.

PDF edition page 10

The strongest objection is straightforward: asking whether someone favors using AI is closer to asking how often they use it than asking whether its output is accurate. Part of the model advantage may reflect that closeness.

Why the objection matters

Restricting the analysis to current users makes the comparison more demanding, but it does not make the questions independent. Use might shape attitudes; useful experiences, work requirements or task mix might shape both. These simultaneous self-reports cannot separate those explanations.

What survives the objection

Accuracy trust alone was an incomplete description of daily-use patterns in the 2025 sample. Attitude and trust should be asked separately. The study does not establish that making people more enthusiastic or more trusting will improve adoption or work quality.

The outcome being predicted matters

The attitude advantage does not transfer uniformly to every outcome. For four-plus-hours use in 2026, context plus attitude and context plus trust were essentially tied on AUC (0.7049 and 0.7046). This outcome has no matching 2025 measure, so it cannot establish a change in that relationship over time.

At the default 0.5 classification cutoff, both 2026 single-question models merely matched always predicting daily: balanced accuracy 0.500, despite above-chance AUC. Useful ranking is different from a useful decision rule. [3]

12. What the four surveys show together.

PDF edition page 13

2023-2025 FOUNDATION

Use rose while enthusiasm and accuracy trust fell.

Current use: 44.4% to 78.5%. Favorability among current users: 84.8% to 72.6%. Accuracy trust: 48.2% to 39.3%. These are annual sample patterns, not individual trajectories.

ATTITUDE AND TRUST

Favorability and trust are not interchangeable answers.

In 2025, 85.1% of very favorable, highly accuracy-distrustful users were daily users. In 2026, 88.9% (184/207) of very favorable users who did not trust AI for most work tasks were daily users. The negative-trust categories mean different things; this is conceptual continuity, not a matched trend.

WITHIN-YEAR MODEL COMPARISONS

Attitude ranks daily use better; trust still adds.

Attitude ranks daily use better in both years; trust improves the combined model in both. The numerical advantage is smaller in 2026, while its trust item adds a larger increment. Changed instruments and samples prevent a claim that either factor became more or less causally important.

USE WITHOUT ENTHUSIASM

Daily use and unfavorable views coexist more visibly.

Very unfavorable daily-use shares were 18.8% and 54.3% under different measures. New questions on trust conditions, policy and hours extend the inquiry. They do not reveal why those people used AI or prove an improvement in their work.

The study did not test the effects of training, trust-building, policy or other adoption strategies. Those causal questions require different evidence. [1-3]

13. Ask five different questions. Measure each directly.

PDF edition page 14

A useful measurement plan separates behavior, attitude and trust from verification burden and work outcomes. The survey findings explain why these questions should be asked separately. They do not establish a universal target for use or trust.

01

Behavior: what is actually being used?

Survey evidence: reported current and daily use. Local evidence to collect: which tasks use AI, how often, and what people actually do with its output.

02

Attitude: how do people feel about using it?

Survey evidence: favorability toward AI use. Ask users what creates value or difficulty. A negative view does not diagnose a training need.

03

Trust: what will people rely on, and when?

2023-2025 asked about accuracy; 2026 asked about conditions and task scope. Neither demonstrates that a particular reliance decision was appropriate.

04

Verification burden: what does checking cost?

Measure review, correction, testing and rework in the chosen workflow. The reported frequency and trust comparisons do not establish that burden or the reasons people accept it.

05

Outcomes: did the work improve?

Inspect actual quality, total time and remaining risks against a baseline. More frequent use and stronger trust do not, by themselves, answer this question.

Proposed aim: reliance that fits the task and evidence. Verification may help a tool earn its place despite imperfect accuracy; that is a hypothesis to test in real work, not an outcome established by this survey. [3]

14. Turn the insight into a decision.

PDF edition page 15

Start with one defined workflow and an accountable owner. These four steps are proposed leadership practices informed by the distinctions in the data, not interventions tested by the surveys.

01

INSPECT THE WORK

Choose one workflow and establish a baseline.

Record task-level use and inspect actual outputs. Measure quality and total time, including checking and correction, against a defined standard.

Keep a baseline work record and name its owner.

02

LISTEN TO DIFFERENT USERS

Walk through real work with the people doing it.

Include daily users with favorable and unfavorable views. Ask what helps, what creates effort, and why they use AI. Do not infer the reason from their attitude.

Record their explanations alongside the work examples.

03

DEFINE ACCEPTABLE RELIANCE

Set task-specific acceptance and verification rules.

Specify what good enough means, what must be checked, and who accepts the result. Include situations where the output should not be relied on.

Write down the checks and the person responsible.

04

MAKE AND REVISIT THE DECISION

Continue, redesign or stop that particular use.

Base the decision on reviewed quality, total effort and remaining risks. Record the reason, assign an owner and review date, and repeat the same measures.

Keep the decision and evidence together.

An unfavorable view is a reason to investigate the work, not an automatic training diagnosis. The surveys provide no universal target for the right amount of use or trust.

15. Keep the comparison honest.

PDF edition page 16

2023-2025: AI-use stems, attitude wording/labels and accuracy-trust wording/labels were stable. In 2025, Yes was divided into daily, weekly and monthly or infrequent use. Combining these categories supports a broad current-use comparison, with a response-format caveat.

2026: use shifted from the development process to tools and agents at work; multi-select tool categories and separate frequencies replaced the earlier use item. Attitude wording/labels and routing changed. Trust became nominal conditions for reliance. The charts visibly break the series at this point.

Rows describe related populations, not a single subtraction chain. Annual attitude/trust charts use item-specific denominators; the primary models require both items.

The four-year evidence separates reported use from attitude and trust; it does not directly measure better work. The practical response is to inspect one workflow, listen to users with different views, define acceptance checks and decide from the resulting evidence. Frequent use alone cannot settle those questions.
