## Why this exists

Between July and August 2026 the company acquired a 13-book press across three
series, and no positioning document was written. The result was drift, not
disagreement — a January content framework still in circulation, four live bios
carrying a retired tagline, and a catalogue nobody had a list of.

This is the first entry in what should become the canonical, versioned source.

## The through-line

> **Recover what already worked. Put it to work on what comes next.**

Theater is the master case, not the whole case. It is a 2,500-year-old craft for
coordinating people under uncertainty — incomplete material, several possible
interpretations, judgment over compliance, meaning made with others — applied to
the newest problem there is.

The Press is the same move in other domains:

| Line | The recovery |
|---|---|
| **Stories That Rehearsed Tomorrow** | People imagined the technology before they had it. Read the rehearsal. |
| **The Past Tests You** | Answer the archive, then judge the archive. |
| **The Forgotten Playbook Library** | Rescue what still works. Retire what does not. |
| **TheaterThink** | An old craft, applied to AI. |
| **Open Research Series** | Test the claim against real data before repeating it. |

One method, several audiences.

## What this changes

**The Press stops being a side venture.** It is evidence that the method
generalises beyond consulting. A practice that says "go and look at what already
worked" and then publishes thirteen books doing exactly that is making its
argument twice.

**It is also the AI answer.** Most of the market forecasts. This position says
the rehearsal already happened and is legible if you go and read it — which is
a defensible claim rather than a prediction, and it is the same standard the
Open Research Series already holds itself to.

**It does not demote Artistic Intelligence.** The philosophy is unchanged:
"what we use when the answer is not waiting to be found." Recovery is where the
raw material comes from; interpretation is still what makes it worth anything.

## Open: "Let's Put On a Show"

A purchasable team experience in which a team has to stage something together
under time pressure and discovers, in the process, how it actually makes
decisions, assigns roles, and handles being unfinished in front of each other.

Where it sits: the bridge between the practice and the Press. It is the
consultancy's method sold as a product, and the Forgotten Playbook Library
already contains adjacent material (Volume 1, 60 cooperative activities, built
on an explicit no-elimination, no-forced-disclosure principle).

### ⚠️ The positioning risk, stated plainly

The Brand Guide's section 2, "What Josh Is Not," lists **"a theater-games
facilitator."** A teambuilding product called *Let's Put On a Show* sits very
close to that category — improv night, trust falls, a crowded market with a
reputation problem the brand has deliberately avoided.

The framing has to carry real weight or the product will be misread:

- **The show is the instrument, not the point.** What is being built is a
  read on how the team actually works when the script runs out.
- **Rehearsal as a way of knowing** — the practice's existing line — is the
  claim, not "fun offsite."
- The Guide's own warning applies: theater language must "do real work, not
  decorate generic business advice."

### Unresolved, and it changes everything downstream

**Facilitated or self-serve?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Facilitated by Josh** | Highest quality, protects the framing, does not scale, competes with keynote time |
| **A kit the team runs itself** | Scales, sellable through the Press, and the Playbook Library already knows how to make exactly this — but the framing is only as good as the instructions, and this is the product most likely to be run badly |
| **Both, staged** | Facilitated first to learn what actually happens in the room, then productise what survives — which is the method applied to the method |

The third is the one consistent with everything else the practice claims. It is
also slower.
